Guide

Everything in Wanderer, in the order you're likely to need it.

The map

Drag to move the map, pinch to zoom, double-tap to zoom in and tap with two fingers to zoom out. The card at the top shows your grid reference, how accurate your position is and your height. Once you move the map away from yourself, the card shows the grid reference of the cross in the middle of the screen instead.

The three buttons on the right are, from the top:

  • Compass. Turns the map so the way you're facing is up. Tap again for north up. The needle always points to north.
  • More (⋯). Your saved routes, the map layers, downloading an area for offline use, and settings.
  • Location. Brings the map back to you and follows you as you move.

Bottom left are the scale bar and the map you're looking at, such as 1:25 000 Explorer. The Explorer map appears as you zoom in and the Landranger map as you zoom out.

Map layers

Open More → Map layers to choose what the map shows. Under Base map, pick the Explorer map (Ordnance Survey's 1:25 000 and 1:50 000 maps), or a cleaner outdoor, road or pale map. Under Overlays, switch on Footpaths to draw the paths Wanderer uses for planning on top of the map, coloured by right of way: green for public footpaths, orange for bridleways and purple for byways. They appear once you zoom in, for areas you've downloaded or planned in. The bottom of the screen shows how many saved routes are on the map, with a button to hide them all.

Search

Tap the magnifying glass beside the grid reference to search for a town, a fell, a lake, a postcode or a grid reference such as NY 215 072. Places with exactly the name you typed come first, then towns, hills and water ahead of streets, nearest first. Tap a result to move the map there; an orange dot marks the spot.

Offline

Any map you have looked at is kept on the phone. To prepare for somewhere without a signal, open More → Download area for offline. A frame appears over the map; move and zoom until the area you want is inside it. Wanderer shows the size of the download before you start. Leave Footpaths for route planning switched on so you can plan routes there offline too. Keep Wanderer open until the download finishes; the screen stays on while it runs.

Recording

Tap the red record button and choose walk, cycle or kayak. The panel at the bottom left shows the time, distance, average speed, ascent and descent, and the time spent moving. The average speed leaves out the time you were stopped.

Lock the phone and put it away: recording carries on, and the figures appear on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island. Pause and resume with the buttons in the panel. The stop button asks whether to save the track or discard it.

The track is saved every few seconds. If Wanderer is closed or the phone switches off, open it again and the recording will be waiting, paused, with a note that it was recovered. Tap play to carry on.

The first time you record, iPhone asks whether Wanderer may use Motion & Fitness. Allow it: that is how the ascent is measured with the barometer.

When you finish, the track goes straight onto the map and its details open: the times, distance, speeds, climb and a height profile. If it looks as though recording carried on after the walk ended, Wanderer offers to trim it first (see Trimming a track).

Planning

Tap Plan at the top of the screen. A crosshair appears in the middle of the map.

  1. Move the map so the crosshair is on your start, and tap the blue flag button.
  2. Move the map to the next point and tap the + button. Carry on point by point.
  3. Tap Done to go back to the map with your route on it, or Cancel to throw it away.

The row of buttons under the plus:

  • Snap to paths. When on, each leg follows the paths between your points, and a point placed close to a path lands on it. When off, legs are straight lines, which suits open fell or water.
  • Walk / Cycle – roads / Cycle – off-road. Tap to change who the route is for. Walking prefers public footpaths and bridleways. Road cycling prefers quiet lanes and cycleways. Off-road cycling prefers bridleways, byways and tracks. The cycling choices never use a public footpath.
  • Undo removes the last point, and Save keeps the route under a name of your choosing.

As the route grows, the distance shows the total climb beside it, and a height profile appears above the buttons, worked out from Ordnance Survey's height data.

A route you've planned stays on the map when you go back to walking, so you can follow it while recording.

Suggestions

In Plan mode, tap Suggest. Choose the kind of trip, a distance, and whether to start from where you are or from the crosshair, then tap Find loops. Wanderer offers three circular routes heading in different directions, each with its length and what it's made of. Tap one to load it into the planner, where you can change it, save it, or tap Done and set off.

If the footpaths for that area aren't on your phone yet, Wanderer says so and offers to download them first.

Following a route

With a planned route on the map in Walk mode, a label near the top shows how far there is to go. Stray more than about 50 metres from the route and it turns orange, saying how far off you are, and the phone gives a short buzz. While you're recording, the same line appears on the lock screen, which lights up when you leave the route.

Wanderer follows the route you've just planned, or, if there isn't one, the saved route shown on the map, when only one is shown.

Saved routes

Open More → Saved routes. Planned routes and recorded tracks are listed together, newest first; the buttons at the top show just one kind. The eye on each row shows or hides it on the map, and any number can be shown at once. Swipe left to delete; swipe right on a planned route to edit it, or on a recorded track to trim it. The share button sends a GPX file.

Tap a row for its details: a map, the figures, a height profile and, for a planned route, an estimated time, worked out from the distance and the climb. From there you can show it on the map, edit or trim it, rename it, share it or delete it.

Editing a planned route reopens it in the planner, where you can add points to the end or take them off with Undo; saving updates the route. Points in the middle can't yet be moved.

Trimming a track

It's easy to forget to stop recording at the end of a walk, and to find the track carries on into the pub or along the road home. When you press stop, Wanderer looks for a long stop at the end, or a spell at car speed, and if it finds one it says when it thinks you finished and offers to trim the track to then.

You can trim any recorded track later, from its details or by swiping right on it in Saved routes. Drag the slider to the moment you finished: the map shows the part you'll keep in purple and the part to be removed in grey. The distance, time and climb are worked out again for what's kept.

Apple Fitness

Wanderer can save each walk, ride or paddle to Apple Health as a workout, with the route, time, distance, climb and estimated calories, so it appears in the Fitness app. It offers this after your first recording; you can also switch it on in Settings, under Health, or add a single track from its details with Add to Apple Fitness. Settings can send all your earlier tracks across in one go. A trimmed track replaces its workout in Health.

On more than one device

If iCloud Drive is on, your routes and tracks are kept in iCloud Drive, in a folder called Wanderer, and appear on every iPhone and iPad signed in to your Apple Account. A route planned on the iPad is there on the iPhone when you set off. Maps, footpaths and heights are downloaded separately on each device. Settings shows where your routes are being kept.

Settings

Open More → Settings.

  • Units. Metric (kilometres, metres, km/h) or imperial (miles, feet, mph).
  • Show on lock screen while recording. Turn off if you'd rather not have the Live Activity.
  • Save to Apple Health & Fitness. See Apple Fitness.
  • Show the welcome screens again, for a reminder of where everything is.
